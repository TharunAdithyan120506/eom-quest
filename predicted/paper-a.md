## 1A | 4 | m1 | fayol
**Swiggy Instamart** runs 300 dark stores. Read the practices below and identify the **Fayol principle** behind each, explaining it briefly.
i) Each picker reports only to the store captain, never to the regional manager directly.
ii) Every SKU has a fixed rack and bin number, and the most experienced packer is placed at the fast-moving counter.
iii) Store captains may approve refunds up to ₹500; anything above goes to the zonal office.
iv) Pickers who suggest a faster picking route get a monthly "Speed Idea" award.

### Model answer
| Practice | Fayol principle | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| i) Reports only to the store captain | **Unity of command** | One subordinate takes orders from **one superior**, avoiding confusion and divided loyalty. |
| ii) Fixed rack per SKU; best packer at the fast counter | **Order**: material + social order | *A place for everything* (material order) and *the right person in the right job* (social order). |
| iii) Refund limit ₹500, higher goes to zonal office | **Centralization & decentralization** | Routine decisions are delegated downward; bigger ones stay at the top. The balance depends on size and subordinates' ability. |
| iv) Award for route ideas | **Initiative** | Encourages employees to act **without being asked**, using non-monetary incentives. |

**Marking tip:** 1 mark per correct principle with a one-line justification quoting the case.

## 1B | 3 | m1 | csr
**Tata Steel** spends on rural health camps, school scholarships and tree planting around Jamshedpur, even though the law requires much less. A shareholder asks why the company spends money that "does not add to profit". Explain **CSR** and give **three drivers** that justify Tata Steel's approach.

### Model answer
**CSR** is a firm's intention, **beyond its legal and economic obligations**, to do the right things and act in ways that are good for society. It treats the firm as a **moral agent**, and may mean accepting **short-term costs** for positive social and environmental change ("corporate citizenship").

**Drivers that justify it:**
1. **Brand equity**: goodwill from community work raises how customers and investors perceive the Tata brand.
2. **Employees and shareholders**: an ethical, caring culture improves employee satisfaction and competence, and responsible investors value it.
3. **Environmental consciousness / global warming**: tree planting addresses environmental impact and meets rising public expectations.

**Answer to the shareholder:** goodwill and trust take decades to build. CSR protects long-term value even if it doesn't raise this year's profit (the socio-economic view rather than the classical "maximize profit only" view).

## 1C | 3 | m2 | plans
Identify the **type of plan** in each statement made by **Infosys** management:
i) "Our purpose is to amplify human potential through technology."
ii) "No employee may carry a personal pen drive into a client delivery bay."
iii) "To raise the operating margin to 22% by March 2027."

### Model answer
| Statement | Type of plan | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| i) | **Purpose / mission** | States the **basic function or task** of the enterprise. |
| ii) | **Rule** | A specific required **action or non-action**, with **no discretion** and no time sequence. |
| iii) | **Objective (goal)** | An **end** to be achieved: verifiable, with a number and a deadline. |

**Watch out:** a rule allows no flexibility; a policy would only *guide* thinking (e.g. "we generally prefer encrypted cloud storage").

## 2B | 3 | m3 | urwick
Identify **Urwick's principle** in each situation and explain it:
i) At **Asian Paints**, the production and marketing departments both work toward one company target of 12% volume growth instead of separate departmental targets.
ii) A plant manager is held responsible for safety, but is not allowed to stop the line when a hazard appears.
iii) Each role at the plant has a written note of results expected, tasks and decision limits.

### Model answer
1. **Unity of objectives**: the structure is effective when everyone contributes to **enterprise objectives**. Without a common goal, departments chase conflicting targets.
2. **Parity of authority and responsibility (violated)**: responsibility **cannot exceed the authority delegated**. The manager needs authority to stop the line if they are responsible for safety.
3. **Functional definition**: every position has a clear definition of **results expected, activities and authority delegated**.

## 2C | 3 | m3 | departmentation2
**Hindustan Unilever** sells soaps, detergents and foods through a sales force spread across India. Propose a **suitable departmentation** for its sales network and give its **merits and limitations**.

### Model answer
**Territorial (geographic) departmentation**: group the sales force into **North / South / East / West regions → zones → areas**, each under a regional manager. This suits a geographically dispersed market with different local needs. Within each region, product lines can sit underneath (a combined approach).

| Merits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Responsibility placed at lower levels | Needs more people with general-manager ability |
| Focus on local markets and problems | Hard to maintain economical central services |
| Better regional coordination and face-to-face contact | Harder for top management to control |
| Economies of local operation; training ground for GMs | Possible duplication across regions |

**Diagram:** National Sales Head → Regional Managers (N/S/E/W) → Zonal Managers → Area Sales Officers.

## 3A | 3 | m3 | urwick+organizing
You run a 60-person **auto-parts machining unit** and choose a **line-and-staff organization**. Explain how authority, advice and decisions work, with a **neat diagram**.

### Model answer
**Line authority** runs in a direct **scalar chain**: Managing Director → Works Manager → Shift Supervisors → Machinists. Line managers **command and decide** on production. **Staff** specialists (quality engineer, HR officer, maintenance planner) **advise and support** the line but give no orders to machinists (unity of command is kept).

**Diagram**
- MD
  - Works Manager ← *staff (dotted links):* Quality Engineer, HR Officer, Maintenance Planner
    - Supervisor, Shift 1 → machinists
    - Supervisor, Shift 2 → machinists

**How it works:** the quality engineer spots a tolerance problem and **advises** the Works Manager, who **decides** and instructs supervisors. This gives the benefit of **specialization** (Urwick) without breaking the **scalar chain** or **unity of command**.

**Limitation:** possible line–staff friction; staff advice may be ignored or delay decisions.

## 3C | 4 | m5 | herzberg
At **Zoho's** support centre, employees say: *"The pay is fair, the office is excellent and our managers are kind, but the work is the same 40 tickets every day; nobody notices good work and there is no path to grow."* Using **Herzberg's two-factor theory**, diagnose the problem and recommend actions.

### Model answer
**Diagnosis:** the **hygiene factors** (pay, working conditions, supervision, relations) are satisfied, so employees are **not dissatisfied**. But the **motivators** are missing, so they are **not motivated**. This is the *high hygiene, low motivation* cell: few complaints, little drive.

| Missing motivator | Evidence | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Work itself** | "same 40 tickets every day" | Job enrichment: rotate across product lines; own complex cases end to end |
| **Recognition** | "nobody notices good work" | Weekly customer-praise shout-outs; awards for resolution quality |
| **Advancement / growth** | "no path to grow" | Career ladder: Agent → Specialist → Team Lead; certification support |
| **Responsibility / achievement** | (implied) | Let senior agents mentor juniors and handle escalations |

**Key line:** improving hygiene further (e.g. more pay) will not motivate; only **content-based motivators** will.

## 4A | 3 | m4 | sources
As the CHRO of **Mahindra & Mahindra**, recommend the **three best internal and three best external sources** for filling (a) plant supervisor and (b) EV battery R&D engineer roles. Justify briefly.

### Model answer
**Internal sources**: for the **plant supervisor** role
1. **Promotion** of experienced senior technicians, who already know the plant (and it motivates staff).
2. **Job posting / notice boards**: an open internal call gives all eligible staff a fair chance.
3. **Transfer** from another Mahindra plant with surplus supervisors.

**External sources**: for **EV battery R&D engineers** (a scarce, specialized skill)
1. **Educational institutes (campus)**: IITs/NITs for fresh talent with strong fundamentals.
2. **Placement / executive search agencies**: for experienced battery specialists.
3. **Employee recommendations (referrals)**: engineers' networks bring trusted candidates.

**Why the split:** internal sources are faster, cheaper and motivating where the skills exist in-house; external sources bring **new skills and ideas** where they don't.

## 4B | 3 | m4 | training

### Model answer

## 4C | 4 | m5 | likert
Quote lines from the case and identify **Likert's four systems**:
**Bajaj Auto** divisions:
- *Line A:* "Targets are announced by the GM; workers who miss them are warned of pay cuts. No one questions decisions."
- *Line B:* "The manager decides, but rewards good workers and sometimes asks senior operators for opinions, under close control."
- *Line C:* "The manager collects ideas from the team before deciding broad matters; operators decide day-to-day scheduling."
- *Line D:* "The team sets its own targets with the manager; information flows freely up, down and sideways; everyone owns the result."

### Model answer
| Line | Quoted evidence | Likert system | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | "warned of pay cuts… no one questions decisions" | **System 1: Exploitative-authoritative** | No trust; motivation by fear and punishment; decisions at the top; downward communication only |
| B | "decides, but rewards… sometimes asks… under close control" | **System 2: Benevolent-authoritative** | Patronizing trust; rewards with some fear; a little upward communication; delegation under close control |
| C | "collects ideas before deciding… operators decide scheduling" | **System 3: Consultative** | Substantial trust; broad policy at the top, specific decisions lower down; two-way communication |
| D | "team sets its own targets… flows freely… everyone owns" | **System 4: Participative** | Complete trust; group decisions; communication in all directions; high teamwork |

**Conclude:** Likert found **System 4** the most productive, so Bajaj should move Lines A and B toward it.

## 5B | 3 | sdl2 | deming+tqm
**[SDL: Quality Management]** Explain **Deming's PDCA cycle** and show how a hospital can use it to reduce patient waiting time.

### Model answer
**PDCA** is Deming's continuous-improvement loop:
1. **Plan**: set an objective (cut OPD waiting from 60 to 30 minutes), find causes (registration bottleneck), decide measures and responsibilities.
2. **Do**: implement on a small scale (online pre-registration in one department) and collect data.
3. **Check**: measure the effect against the target; analyse deviations.
4. **Act**: if it works, **standardize** it across all departments and document it; if not, learn and re-plan.

The cycle **repeats**: each Act feeds the next Plan, giving continuous improvement (*kaizen*). Draw it as a circle: Plan → Do → Check → Act → back to Plan.

## 5C | 4 | m5 | controltypes+controlprocess
**IndiGo Airlines** wants on-time performance and customer satisfaction. Explain how **feedforward, concurrent and feedback controls** can be used, with examples, and state **two standards** IndiGo could set.

### Model answer
| Control | When | IndiGo example |
|---|---|---|
| **Feedforward** | **Before** operations: anticipates problems | Predictive aircraft maintenance; extra ground staff rostered before festival rush; weather planning |
| **Concurrent** | **During** operations: immediate correction | Live turnaround dashboard; gate supervisors fixing boarding delays as they occur |
| **Feedback** | **After** output: customer reactions | Post-flight satisfaction surveys and complaint analysis used to change processes |

**Standards (reference points for evaluating results):**
- **Time standard**: 90% of flights depart within 15 minutes of schedule.
- **Managerial / quality standard**: customer satisfaction score of at least 4.2/5; or a **monetary** standard such as cost per seat-km.

**Link to the process:** set standards → measure → compare → identify deviations → analyse causes → program and implement corrections.
