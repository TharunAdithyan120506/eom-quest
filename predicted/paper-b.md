Mention the **role category** and **specific Mintzberg role** for each activity of **Nandan, plant head at Maruti Suzuki, Manesar**:
i) Inaugurates the plant's new paint shop with the district collector.
ii) Reads industry reports and chats with suppliers to track chip shortages.

### Model answer
| Activity | Category | Specific role | Why |
|---|---|---|---|

## 2A | 4 | m2 | bcg
**ITC** has four businesses: (i) **cigarettes**, the market leader in a slow-growing market; (ii) **packaged foods (Aashirvaad, Sunfeast)**, a strong player in a fast-growing market; (iii) **personal care (Savlon, Fiama)**, a small share in a fast-growing market; (iv) **paperboards for a declining stationery segment**, a small share in a shrinking market. Place each in the **BCG matrix** and recommend a strategy, with a diagram.

### Model answer
| Business | Share / growth | BCG cell | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cigarettes | High / low | **Cash cow** | **Harvest / hold**: milk the cash to fund others |
| Packaged foods | High / high | **Star** | **Build / hold**: keep investing to keep leadership |
| Personal care | Low / high | **Question mark** | **Build selectively**: invest heavily to gain share, or drop |
| Stationery paperboards | Low / low | **Dog** | **Divest** or harvest; a cash trap |

**Diagram:** a 2×2 grid. Vertical axis: **market growth rate** (high at top). Horizontal axis: **relative market share** (high on the left). Star top-left, Question mark top-right, Cash cow bottom-left, Dog bottom-right.

**Logic:** cash from the cow funds the star and the question mark (portfolio balance). **Limitation:** only two dimensions; ignores synergy between businesses.

## 3A | 3 | m3 | urwick+organizing
**Line organization and delegation.** You founded a 25-person bakery chain with a **pure line structure**. Explain how authority and communication flow, and the steps you would follow to **delegate** order handling to a store manager.

### Model answer
**Line organization:** authority flows in a direct **scalar chain**, Owner → Store Managers → Bakers / Counter staff. Each person has **one boss** (unity of command); communication travels up and down the same chain; decisions are quick and responsibility is clear. *(Draw a simple vertical tree.)*
*Limitation:* no specialists; the owner can become overloaded as the business grows.

**Delegation steps:**
1. **Decide the expected results**: e.g. all online orders confirmed within 10 minutes.
2. **Assign the task**: order handling to the store manager.
3. **Delegate enough authority**: accept or decline orders, offer substitutes up to ₹200.
4. **Hold accountable and review**: daily order report; the owner stays ultimately responsible.

These follow Urwick's principles of **delegation** and **parity of authority and responsibility**.

## 3C | 4 | m5 | maslow+xy
At **Wipro's** night-shift BPO, a manager says: *"These people are lazy; I watch them every minute and threaten deductions."* Employees complain: *"Our transport home at 3 AM is unsafe, and we have no friends here because teams change every week."* Analyse using **McGregor** and **Maslow**, and recommend actions.

### Model answer
**McGregor:** the manager holds **Theory X** assumptions (people dislike work and need close control and threats). That leads to an autocratic style and tight control, which worsens morale. Shift to **Theory Y**: trust, self-direction and responsibility.

**Maslow (unmet needs from the quotes):**
| Quote | Unmet need | Action |
|---|---|---|
| "transport home at 3 AM is unsafe" | **Safety** | Safe cab with GPS and escort; security policy |
| "no friends… teams change every week" | **Social (belonging)** | Stable teams, team events |
| (constant watching, threats) | **Esteem** | Recognition, participation in decisions |

**Logic:** lower needs (safety, then social) must be met first before higher needs motivate.

## 4A | 3 | m4 | recruitment+selection
**Flipkart** is hiring 2,000 delivery associates before Big Billion Days. Explain the **selection process** it should use after receiving applications.

### Model answer
1. **Preliminary interview**: quick screening of basic eligibility (licence, vehicle, area).
2. **Application form**: identity, education, experience and expected pay recorded.
3. **Reference checks**: past employers or known persons confirm reliability.
4. **Employment tests**: e.g. route-navigation and app-usage test (aptitude/skill), driving check.
5. **Interview**: a structured interview on customer handling and availability.
6. **Selection decision**: choose the best fit and keep **runners-up** in case some decline.

*(Recruitment attracts candidates; selection screens them down to the best fit.)*

## 5C | 4 | m5 | controlprocess
Explain the **steps in controlling** with a **neat diagram**, using **Amul's** milk-collection quality as the example, and name the **types of standards** involved.

### Model answer
**Steps (draw as a loop):**
1. **Establish standards**: fat ≥ 4.5%, SNF ≥ 8.5%, collection by 7 AM.
2. **Actual performance**: milk is collected at village centres.
3. **Measure** actual performance: lab testing at the chilling centre.
4. **Compare** with the standards.
5. **Identify deviations**: 6% of cans below the fat standard.
6. **Analyse causes**: adulteration at two centres; delayed chilling.
7. **Program corrective action**: surprise checks, faster chilling vans, farmer training.
8. **Implement** corrections, then loop back to standards.

**Standards involved:** **physical/technical** (fat %, SNF), **time** (collection by 7 AM), **monetary** (cost per litre). *(Optionally note: this is mainly feedback control, with chilling-van planning as feedforward.)*
