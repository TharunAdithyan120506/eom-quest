## hrm
### HRM vs HRD
- **HRM (Human Resource Management)** manages **people within the organization** and handles administrative tasks, policies and procedures.
- **HRD (Human Resource Development)** **enhances employees' skills, knowledge and capabilities** through learning and development.

HRM is the **bigger concept**: it covers planning, staffing, developing, monitoring, maintaining, managing relationships and evaluating. HRD covers the **development part**: training, learning, career development, talent management, performance appraisal, engagement and empowerment.

| Basis | HRM | HRD |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Managing human resources | Developing employees |
| Scope | Broad (includes HRD) | Narrower, a part of HRM |
| Functions | HR planning, job analysis, recruitment, selection, placement, induction & orientation | Training & development, career development, organizational development |
| Nature | Largely administrative and maintenance | Growth and learning oriented |

### Benefits of integrating HRM and HRD
1. **Holistic employee development**: immediate support plus long-term skill growth.
2. **Strategic alignment**: HR practices directly support organizational goals.
3. **Improved productivity**: better skills and engagement raise efficiency.
4. **Enhanced talent retention**: investing in people builds loyalty and cuts turnover.

### Functions of HRM
HRM functions are divided into **managerial functions** (planning, organizing, directing, controlling the HR work) and **operative functions** (procurement, development, compensation, integration and maintenance of people).

### Key definitions
- **Job**: a group of tasks that must be performed for the organization to achieve its goals.
- **Position**: the tasks and responsibilities performed by **one person**; there is a position for every individual.
- **Job analysis**: the systematic process of determining the **skills, duties and knowledge** required for jobs.
- **Job description**: a document on the job's **tasks, duties and responsibilities**.
- **Job specification**: the **minimum qualifications** a person needs to do the job.

## jobanalysis
### Job analysis
The **systematic process of determining the skills, duties and knowledge** required to perform jobs. It answers: What skills are needed? What qualification? What abilities are desirable? What experience? What responsibilities?

Job analysis produces two documents:
| Job description (about the **job**) | Job specification (about the **person**) |
|---|---|
| **Tasks, duties, responsibilities** | **Knowledge, skills, abilities** |
| Job title, department, reporting line, purpose, duties, working conditions | Education, experience, skills, personal and physical requirements |

### Where job analysis is used
Human resource planning · recruitment · selection · training and development · performance appraisal · compensation and benefits · safety and health · employee and labour relations · legal considerations · job analysis for teams.

### Slide examples
- *Centre for Policy Research, Librarian*: **specification** = post-graduate degree in library science, five years as assistant/deputy librarian, computer knowledge, M.Phil/Ph.D preferred.
- *"The best job in the world", island caretaker*: **description** = clean the pool, feed the fish, collect the mail, explore and report back.

### Writing one in the exam (e.g. a fresher role)
**Job description**: title · department · reports to · job purpose (one line) · 4–6 key duties · working conditions.
**Job specification**: essential qualification · experience (fresher / 0–1 yr) · technical skills · soft skills · personal attributes.

> **Memory:** *Description = the Duties. Specification = the Skills (of the person).*

## recruitment
### Recruitment → selection → placement
1. **Recruitment**: searching for **prospective employees** who suit the job specification.
2. **Selection**: choosing the **right person** from among the applicants.
3. **Placement**: putting the selected person on the job **most suited** to them.

### What is recruitment?
The process of **locating, identifying and attracting capable candidates**, for current or future needs. It is a critical activity for many corporations. The **recruitment process** is a strategic framework to find and attract the best candidates; details vary by industry.

### The 10-step recruitment process
1. **Identifying hiring needs**: is there a vacancy or new role?
2. **Job analysis and job description**: define the role.
3. **Sourcing candidates**: advertise, use portals, referrals, campuses.
4. **Screening and shortlisting**: filter applications.
5. **Interviews**.
6. **Assessments and tests**.
7. **Background checks**.
8. **Decision making**.
9. **Offer and negotiation**.
10. **Onboarding**: welcome and integrate the new hire.

> **Memory story:** *We **need** someone → write the **job** → **source** people → **screen** them → **interview** → **test** → **check** backgrounds → **decide** → **offer** → **onboard**.*

## sources
### Types of recruitment
1. **Internal**: hiring from within for a vacancy.
2. **External**: sourcing from outside.
3. **Campus**: fresh graduates directly from universities.
4. **Executive search**: specialized hiring for senior/executive roles.
5. **Contingent**: agencies paid **only if** they fill the position.
6. **Remote**: hiring people to work outside a traditional office.
7. **Diversity and inclusion**: strategies to build a diverse workforce.
8. **Referral**: candidates recommended by current employees or contacts.
9. **Social media**: LinkedIn, Twitter, Facebook.
10. **Full-cycle**: covering every stage from sourcing to onboarding.
11. **Digital** recruitment.

### Challenges in recruitment
Talent shortage · high competition · time and resource constraints · evolving job market and skills gap · diversity and inclusion · candidate experience · technology and automation · compliance and regulations · retention and turnover · market and industry dynamics.

### Sources of recruitment
| Internal sources | External sources |
|---|---|
| Promotion | Press advertisements |
| Transfer | Educational institutes (campus) |
| Demotion | Placement agencies |
| Retired employees | Employment agencies (exchanges) |
| Notice boards | Unsolicited applicants |
| Job posting | Employee recommendations |

### Choosing between them
- **Internal**: faster and cheaper, motivates staff (promotion chances), candidates already know the culture; but limits fresh ideas and leaves a chain of vacancies.
- **External**: brings new skills and ideas and a wider choice; but costlier, slower and riskier, and can demotivate insiders.

> **Exam approach:** for a senior specialized role → executive search / external; for a growing role with good insiders → promotion / job posting; for mass entry-level hiring → campus recruitment and job portals. Justify each choice from the case.

## selection
### What is selection?
**Picking, from among the candidates, the one who best meets the position's requirements.** Recruitment is *positive* (attract many); selection *screens out* until the best fit remains.

### The six steps of selection
1. **Initial / preliminary interview**: a first meeting to check **basic qualifications and fit**.
2. **Application form**: gives factual information and becomes part of the employee's records: identity, education, experience, expected salary and benefits, community activities.
3. **Reference checks**: names of former employers and two or three unrelated people who know the candidate; done properly, they reveal what interviews and tests can't.
4. **Employment tests**: instruments that **measure selected qualities** against the job specification; a sample of behaviour used to predict future performance.
5. **Interview**: the main in-depth assessment.
6. **Selection decision**: the hiring manager picks the best fit after feedback; often a **runner-up** is kept in case the first choice declines or fails later testing/training.

### Types of employment tests (measures of human psychology)
| Category | Tests |
|---|---|
| **Aptitude** | Intelligence/mental, emotional quotient, skill, aptitude, psychomotor, clerical aptitude |
| **Achievement** | Job knowledge, work sample |
| **Situational** | Group discussion, in-basket |
| **Interest** | Interest tests |
| **Personality** | Objective, projective, situation tests |
| **Multi-dimensional** | Combined tests |

### Types of interviews
- **Preliminary**: informal, unstructured.
- **Core**: background-information, job and probing, **stress**, group-discussion, formal/structured, **panel**, **depth** interviews.
- **Decision-making** interview (final).

> **Memory:** *Meet → fill → check → test → talk → decide.*

## training
### What is training?
**Acquiring specific knowledge and skills for a particular job or task.** It is usually **short-term** and aims to improve **current** job performance: formal courses, on-the-job training or coaching. (*Development* is longer-term growth for future roles.)

### The five-stage training & development process
1. **Needs assessment**: find the gap between required and actual skills.
2. **Design and development**: set objectives, content and methods.
3. **Delivery**: run the training.
4. **Evaluation**: did learning and performance improve?
5. **Follow-up**: reinforce and support transfer to the job.

### Current trends
1. **Digital learning**
2. **Personalized training programmes**
3. **Microlearning** (short, focused units)
4. **Gamification**
5. **Diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI) training**
6. **Soft-skills development**

### Company examples from the slides
- **Microsoft**: technical training, leadership and career workshops; an annual **Hackathon** for collaborative ideas.
- **Google**: leadership, technical and wellness programmes; employees **teach and learn from each other**.
- **Amazon**: extensive on-the-job training; **Career Choice** pays 95% of tuition for high-demand fields.
- **Starbucks**: the **Starbucks Experience**: online and in-person training on customer service, coffee and drink preparation, with continuous coaching.
- **Walmart**: customer service, compliance and leadership training plus **tuition assistance**.

## trainingmethods
### On-the-job vs off-the-job methods
| On-the-job (learn while working) | Off-the-job (learn away from the workplace) |
|---|---|
| **Apprenticeship** programmes | **Conferences** |
| **Coaching** | **Films** |
| **Internship** training | **Case study** |
| **Job rotation** | **Computer modelling** |
| | **Vestibule training** (a replica of the work setup) |
| | **Programmed instruction** |

**Choosing a method:** on-the-job suits practical skills learnt with real equipment; off-the-job suits concepts, risky equipment, or when work can't be disrupted.

### Induction vs orientation
| Induction | Orientation |
|---|---|
| The **formal process of introducing** a new employee to the **organization**, its policies and culture | A **structured programme** to familiarize new employees with their **job role, team and work environment** |
| Organization-wide, "welcome to the company" | Job-specific, "here is your role and team" |

### Sensitivity training
Training that makes people **more aware of their own goals and prejudices**, and **more sensitive to others** and to the dynamics of group interaction.

## staffing
### The systems approach to staffing (draw this diagram!)
Staffing is seen as a **system** linked to the rest of management, inside the **external environment** and shaped by the **internal environment** (personnel policies and reward system).

**Flow of the model:**
1. **Enterprise plans** → **organization plans**
2. → the **number and kinds of managers required**
3. compared with the **manager inventory** (who we already have)
4. → **analysis of present and future needs for managers**
5. → filled from **external sources** and **internal sources**
6. → **recruitment, selection, placement, promotion, separation**
7. → **appraisal, career strategy, training and development**
8. → **leading and controlling**

**Dashed feedback lines** connect every box back to the plans and to the internal environment.

> **Drawing tip:** a big outer box labelled *External environment*; two stacked boxes on the left (*managers required*, *manager inventory*) meeting at *analysis of needs*; it splits into *external/internal sources*, rejoins at *recruitment… separation*, then *appraisal/training*, then *leading & controlling*; a dashed line along the bottom labelled *Internal environment: personnel policies, reward system*.

## managerdevelopment
### Developing excellent managers: eight considerations
1. **Instilling a willingness to learn**
2. **Accelerating management development**
3. **Planning for innovation**
4. **Measuring and rewarding management effectively**
5. **Tailoring information** to managers' needs
6. **Expanding research and development in tools and techniques**
7. **Developing more managerial inventories**
8. **Creating strong intellectual leadership**

> **Memory:** start with the *person* (learn, accelerate), then the *system* (innovation, rewards, information), then the *organization's capability* (tools, inventories, intellectual leadership).
